Toki Pona and Concept Resolution

Those who say Toki Pona can describe everything are missing the point. Those who say Toki Pona cannot describe everything are also missing the point.

When one learns a concept, it is linked to related concepts. Therefore, one can describe a concept using other concepts. For example, the word "computer", by its composition, is linked to the idea of computing. In fact, there is also a hidden level of indirection from the word "computer" to the object itself. The fewer levels of indirection there are, the faster one can resolve a description to the thing it describes. Therefore, the most efficient way to describe something is through a symbol that is directly and exclusively connected to it. This is the reason why we create new terms, symbols that are not (though not necessarily cannot) broken down into simpler components for understanding: "computer" is much quicker to understand than, say, "a programmable electronic device that performs mathematical calculations and logical operations and can process, store and retrieve large amounts of data very quickly". In fact, many descriptions became terms or are effectively ones: For example, "laser" came from "Light Amplification through Stimulated Emission of Radiation", but one does not expand the acronym in one's head while reading the word.

From what I have read, most tokiponists generally oppose lexicalization for two reasons. The first one is that it goes against the premise of Toki Pona, which is that one explains everything using a minimal set of terms. There are countless different concepts, so assigning each concept a new term is anything but minimalist. The second one is that assigning a specific, narrow meaning to a phrase will replace that phrase's normal meaning, which makes it harder to express general things, to the extreme of only being able to talk about specific concepts, while the words simply form some sort of ID system that label different concepts, losing any meaning on their own. These are both valid! Adhering to Toki Pona's spirit, one should indeed not lexicalize terms. However, this leads people who want to talk about advanced concepts to complain that it is impossible, or requires way too many words. Those who do succeed end up "locally lexicalizing" phrases to mean certain concepts. While these phrases, by Toki Pona's philosophy, is only a wording, chosen by the author, within the context of the scope of the work, popular articles and videos often end up creating de facto "standard wordings" for concepts, effectively lexicalizing certain phrases, at least within a subject.

I think that it is wrong to say that Toki Pona cannot be used to explain advanced concepts (as shown, it can), but it is also wrong to say that Toki Pona can be easily used to explain them, for talking about advanced concepts in Toki Pona is inefficient, and should not be encouraged. This is not to say that Toki Pona itself is inefficient, as some seem to then claim: it is efficient with its goals in mind. Notably, "having an easy and efficient way of talking about advanced concepts" is not one of Toki Pona's goals, and one can imagine that the description of abstract and complex things was not one of the top optimization priorities in the design of this language. One can definitely make a video game in Verilog, but doing it not for printing an ASIC, but for playing the game on a simulator is probably a huge waste of time. Similarly, attempting to talking about these complex concepts using Toki Pona is not impossible, but is, in fact, using the wrong tool for the job. Yes, this means that Toki Pona cannot replace all other languages, and in fact, it would be extremely inefficient for any language to be used for everything.
