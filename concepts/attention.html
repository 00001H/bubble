Attention, the overlooked counterpart to time

Time is often deemed one of, if not the most important resource. It is strictly limited in supply, meaning saving time is often of high priority. Additionally, it is easily measurable, meaning time saves are tangible and goals are easily set. However, its intangible counterpart, attention, is often overlooked.

The importance of attention is never understated. For example, in Chinese, a tedious thing or task is often said to "浪费时间精力", or to waste time and attention. However, because attention is not easily measured, people often think it is an infinite resource, that we just have to "pay" it when we need to, and there's no such thing as "running out of attention". In fact, there is, and we run into it every day. We need to sleep at night, but maybe for more time than we need to. If we tried to conserve attention, we could easily regularly cut that sleeping time by at least an hour for a few days every month or so (if you don't already sleep for less than 5 hours a day, of course). We can trade attention for time, but many are too tired to do so, and this is precisely because they wasted too much attention.

What wastes attention?

Many things waste attention, but often in subtle ways. The most common source of attention drain is excess information, which serve only as distractions. When we receive information that we already know, this is wasted attention. While we would not spend too much time on useless information and just glance over them, it still takes a bit of subconscious effort to realize that the information is, in fact, useless. For example: People who use touch typing, the labels on the keys are completely useless and serve only as a distraction, though in this case, the distraction is rather minor, as the keyboard takes up little to no space in our vision when we are reading screens.
