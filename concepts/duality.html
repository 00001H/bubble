The Two Parts Of A Human

Often, we contrast "rational thinking" with "emotional thinking", but this is very misleading. Most, if not all, tasks require both. For example, a person takes vaccines to reduce their probability of being sick, we would conventionally classify this as rational thinking. However, the assumption here that not being sick is a good thing is based on emotions. Without emotions, a person would be indifferent towards sickness, and even death, and thus there would be no reason for them to take a vaccine.

Thus, we observe that any (non-instinctive) action must come in two parts: first, a person must have an emotion-driven desire (e.g. not wanting to be sick or die); then, they use rational thinking to find a way to reach that goal (e.g. taking a vaccine). The Bubble is concerned with the optimization of the latter, and clear separation of the two parts can be very helpful in optimizing one's actions.
